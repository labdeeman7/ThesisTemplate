\section*{Chapter Introduction}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Chapter Introduction}

This chapter establishes the literature foundation for the thesis. It examines multitask learning as a framework for combining the related perceptual and semantic tasks required for minimally invasive surgical scene understanding. In the wider thesis narrative, the review motivates the progression from separate recognition tasks towards instance-level localisation, grounded interaction modelling, and multimodal interaction prediction.

The main body of the chapter reproduces the accepted manuscript of Alabi, Vercauteren, and Shi, \emph{Multitask Learning in Minimally Invasive Surgical Vision: A Review}, published in \emph{Medical Image Analysis} (2025). The manuscript text is retained essentially unchanged. Changes are limited to the LaTeX integration required by the thesis template, including heading context, figure paths, cross-reference isolation, and use of the thesis-wide bibliography.

\subsection*{Abstract}

Minimally invasive surgery (MIS) has revolutionized many procedures and led to reduced recovery time and risk of patient injury. However, MIS poses additional complexity and burden on surgical teams. Data-driven surgical vision algorithms are thought to be key building blocks in the development of future MIS systems with improved autonomy. Recent advancements in machine learning and computer vision have led to successful applications in analyzing videos obtained from MIS with the promise of alleviating challenges in MIS videos.

Surgical scene and action understanding encompasses multiple related tasks that, when solved individually, can be memory-intensive, inefficient, and fail to capture task relationships. Multitask learning (MTL), a learning paradigm that leverages information from multiple related tasks to improve performance and aid generalization, is well-suited for fine-grained and high-level understanding of MIS data.

This review provides a narrative overview of the current state-of-the-art MTL systems that leverage videos obtained from MIS. Beyond listing published approaches, we discuss the benefits and limitations of these MTL systems. Moreover, this manuscript presents an analysis of the literature for various application fields of MTL in MIS, including those with large models, highlighting notable trends, new directions of research, and developments.

\section*{Chapter Summary and Transition}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Chapter Summary and Transition}

The review shows that surgical scene understanding is inherently multitask: instruments, anatomy, workflow, actions, and their relationships describe complementary aspects of the same operative scene. It also identifies a practical limitation that shapes the next stage of this thesis. Fine-grained and grounded interaction understanding requires reliable spatial supervision, yet large-scale instance-level annotations of laparoscopic instruments have historically been limited.

The next chapter addresses this data bottleneck through CholecInstanceSeg, providing instrument instance masks that support the later transition from frame-level recognition to spatially grounded surgical action triplets.
