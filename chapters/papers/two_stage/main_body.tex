\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:c5:introduction}

The preceding chapters develop a progression from individual surgical-vision tasks to spatially grounded instrument--tissue interactions. Chapter~\ref{chap:background} identifies the need to connect complementary perceptual and semantic information; Chapter~\ref{chap:cholecinstanceseg} provides the instrument-instance annotations required to distinguish individual tools; and Chapter~\ref{chap:triplet-grounding} combines those spatial annotations with surgical action triplets to establish CholecTriplet-Seg and TargetFusionNet. These chapters establish that individual instrument instances can be localised reliably. Assigning the correct verb and anatomical target to each instance is more difficult, with target prediction remaining the main source of error.

This imbalance motivates a two-stage formulation. Instrument instance segmentation is a dense prediction problem for which specialist architectures are well suited. Once the instruments have been localised, interaction prediction is instead a structured semantic task: the model must interpret the action, anatomy, and surrounding scene for each known instance. Requiring one architecture to learn both capabilities jointly may not be necessary. Separating them also provides a controlled setting in which interaction prediction can first be studied with ground-truth instrument instances and then evaluated as part of a complete pipeline with predicted instances.

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) offer a natural candidate for the second stage because they combine visual input with textual instructions and can generate structured outputs. This chapter investigates whether an MLLM can serve as a viable interaction predictor when instrument instances are supplied, rather than asking it to perform dense localisation. The framework, summarised in Figure~\ref{fig:c5:framework}, uses a fixed Mask2Former~\cite{cheng2022masked} for instrument grounding and Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct~\cite{bai2025qwen3vl} for \vtpair prediction. The complete triplet is formed by combining the fixed instrument identity from Stage~1 with the interaction predicted by Stage~2.

Importantly, Stage~2 is not formulated as conventional frame-level surgical triplet recognition. Frame-level recognition determines whether a triplet occurs somewhere in an image, but need not associate it with a particular physical instrument when several instances are visible. Here, each supplied instance is identified by a mask overlay and a unique textual identifier. The overlay can be viewed as a visual prompt that directs the MLLM to the instrument for which an interaction must be predicted. Stage~2 predicts a \vtpair interaction for each identifier, preserving the association between the semantic prediction and its instrument mask. The complete system therefore produces spatially grounded triplet instances rather than an unordered set of frame-level triplet labels.

The study first establishes Direct supervised fine-tuning (Direct SFT) as the principal baseline. In this formulation, the MLLM is fine-tuned to predict the verb and target together in one concise structured response from the visually prompted image and textual instruction. The study then evaluates controlled changes to this second-stage formulation. Sequential Dialogue Prediction first predicts the verb, then predicts the target using the verb response as context, and finally returns the complete triplet; temporal variants supply short windows of neighbouring frames; an anatomical-context variant supplies coarse EndoViT-derived evidence around an estimated instrument tip; ontology-guided prompting supplies valid \vtpair combinations; and distilled-reasoning supervision uses rationales generated by a larger teacher model. These variants are evaluated as individual adaptations, not as a factorial or cumulative ablation study.

The chapter addresses four research questions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Can an MLLM serve as a viable second-stage interaction predictor when instrument instances are provided?
    \item How do sequential factorisation, short temporal context, anatomical priors, ontology guidance, and distilled-reasoning supervision affect interaction prediction?
    \item Can the complete two-stage system remain competitive when the instrument instances are predicted?
    \item Which limitations prevent the observed differences from being interpreted as definitive improvements?
\end{enumerate}

The principal contribution is the two-stage formulation and its empirical evaluation. Direct SFT, which predicts the verb and target together in one response, provides a strong baseline. Sequential Dialogue Prediction, which predicts the verb before using it to inform the target prediction, and three-frame temporal context give promising complete-triplet results under ground-truth grounding. In contrast, the other evaluated sources of context and longer generated supervision do not consistently improve performance. The chapter further demonstrates that stronger component metrics do not necessarily yield a commensurate increase in complete-triplet AP because the instrument, verb, and target must all be correct for the same instance.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:c5:related-work}

\subsection{Grounded Surgical Triplet Prediction}

Surgical action triplets represent an interaction as the \ivttriplet tuple. TripNet introduced triplet recognition as a frame-level task, while Rendezvous subsequently modelled relationships between instruments, verbs, and targets through attention mechanisms~\cite{1nywoye_rec_action_triplets,NWOYE2022102433_rendevous}. Later work investigated temporal modelling, self-distillation, and multi-task optimisation, including MT4MTL-KD and TERL~\cite{gui2023mt4mtl,gui2024tail}. These methods improved frame-level recognition but did not directly associate every interaction with a precise instrument instance.

A closely related alternative is the Multi-class Instrument-aware Transformer--Interaction Graphs (MCIT-IG) framework~\cite{sharma2023surgical}. MCIT-IG localises instruments and represents their interactions using a bipartite graph in which action relationships form edges between instrument and target representations. It therefore addresses instance-associated surgical triplet prediction through explicit graph-based relational modelling, using a heuristic to resolve data association under weak triplet supervision.

Chapter~\ref{chap:triplet-grounding} introduces CholecTriplet-Seg and triplet segmentation with mask-level annotations~\cite{alabi2026grounding}. Its Mask2Former-Triplet baseline and TargetFusionNet jointly learn instrument localisation and semantic triplet labels, with TargetFusionNet incorporating weak anatomical priors to support target prediction. The present chapter retains the same grounded task and evaluation protocol but separates instrument instance segmentation from interaction prediction. This follows a two-stage principle related to MCIT-IG, but uses instrument instance masks as visual prompts to an MLLM rather than representing interactions through a scene graph.

\subsection{Multimodal Models in Surgical Vision}

MLLMs have increasingly been studied for surgical visual question answering, dialogue, and scene interpretation. SurgicalGPT combines visual representations with an autoregressive language model for surgical visual question answering, while Surgical-LLaVA adapts instruction-tuned vision--language models to surgical images and videos~\cite{seenivasan2023surgicalgpt,jin2024surgical}. Related triplet-recognition work has also used MLLM-generated semantic descriptions as auxiliary information within discriminative models~\cite{zhang2025mejo}. In contrast, this chapter uses an MLLM itself as the structured interaction-prediction stage after instrument instance segmentation.

\section{Two-Stage Multimodal Interaction Prediction}
\label{sec:c5:method}

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:c5:problem}

Let $I\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times3}$ denote a laparoscopic image. Stage~1 produces a set of grounded instrument instances
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{S}=\{s_i\}_{i=1}^{N}, \qquad s_i=(m_i,c_i,\mathrm{id}_i),
    \label{eq:c5:grounded-instances}
\end{equation}
where $s_i$ denotes the $i$th physical instrument instance in the image, $m_i$ is its binary mask, $c_i$ is its fixed instrument category, and $\mathrm{id}_i$ is the within-image textual identifier used to associate the model output with that instance. Stage~2 predicts
\begin{equation}
    f_{\theta}(I,\mathcal{S})\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}, \qquad
    \mathcal{Y}=\{(v_i,t_i)\}_{i=1}^{N},
    \label{eq:c5:interaction-mapping}
\end{equation}
where $v_i$ and $t_i$ denote the verb and target. The final triplet for instance $i$ is $\tau_i=(c_i,v_i,t_i)$. Stage~2 receives the instrument identity as a fixed input and cannot revise it.

The index $i$ preserves the correspondence between each predicted interaction and the supplied grounded instance. Thus, although Stage~2 performs semantic interaction prediction, its output remains instance-specific: for example, it predicts the interaction associated with \texttt{grasper\_2}, not merely that a grasper interaction occurs somewhere in the frame.

Two grounding settings are evaluated for different purposes. Ground-truth grounding supplies the annotated instrument masks, categories, and instance count, allowing the Stage~2 interaction predictor and its adaptations to be evaluated independently of localisation errors. Predicted grounding instead supplies the outputs of Stage~1 and evaluates the complete two-stage system under realistic error propagation. False-positive Stage~1 instances are passed to Stage~2, whereas missed instruments are absent and cannot be recovered.

\subsection{Stage 1: Fixed Instrument Instance Segmentation}

Stage~1 uses the Mask2Former architecture, backbone, augmentation, optimisation, checkpoint-selection criterion, and inference configuration described for instrument instance segmentation in Chapter~\ref{chap:cholecinstanceseg}. For the present study, it is retrained on the CholecTriplet-Seg training split, including the corrected instrument masks available for this task-specific subset. The detector is fixed throughout all Chapter~5 experiments. Predicted masks are retained using the established Mask2Former confidence filtering and are passed to Stage~2 without refinement. Stage~1 therefore supplies only the masks, instrument categories, and instance count required to construct the Stage~2 input.

\subsection{Stage 2: Structured Interaction Prediction}

Mask-based visual prompt construction converts the grounded instances into the visual input consumed by Stage~2. For single-frame experiments, every instrument mask is rendered translucently onto the original RGB image. This representation communicates instance localisation while preserving the surrounding surgical context within a single visual input. During method development, supplying the original and annotated images separately increased visual-token usage, training time, and memory requirements, while serialising mask polygons as text produced long coordinate sequences that distracted from the semantic prediction task. The single-image overlay was therefore adopted as a compact visual prompt.

Each instance additionally receives a bounding box and a textual identifier formed from its instrument category and a within-frame instance ID, such as \texttt{hook\_1} or \texttt{grasper\_2}. Instrument categories have fixed colours; multiple instances of the same category share a colour and are distinguished by their identifiers. Instance IDs follow the order in which instances are received from the annotation or detector, are unique only within the frame, and have no semantic meaning. The same mask-based visual prompt construction is used for ground-truth and predicted grounding; only the source of the masks, categories, and instance count differs.

As Qwen3-VL is an instruction-tuned generative model, the mask-based visual prompt is paired with a textual instruction that defines the interaction-prediction task. The instruction lists the permitted verb and target labels, identifies the instrument instances that must be labelled, and requests a predefined JSON response schema. This converts otherwise open-ended generation into structured fixed-vocabulary prediction, although the output is not constrained programmatically during decoding. A single-instance response has the form:

\begin{lstlisting}[style=c5prompt]
{
  "predictions": [
    {"id": "grasper_1", "instrument": "Grasping Forceps",
     "verb": "retract", "target": "gallbladder"}
  ]
}
\end{lstlisting}

The instrument category is repeated to serialise the complete triplet, but it is supplied by Stage~1 and cannot be revised by Stage~2. The label vocabulary includes the \texttt{null\_verb} and \texttt{null\_target} categories introduced in \chapref{chap:triplet-grounding}. All instances in an image are processed jointly in one autoregressive response. At inference, the generated JSON is parsed, predictions are matched to grounded instances using the \texttt{id} field, and the \texttt{verb} and \texttt{target} strings are mapped to the corresponding evaluation labels. Generation remains unconstrained: Direct SFT does not enforce valid triplets during decoding or apply ontology-based filtering. Parsing and AP evaluation occur after generation and are not part of the training objective. Malformed objects, invalid labels, duplicate identifiers, and omitted instances are treated as missing predictions and receive no credit. Complete instructions and response schemas are provided in Section~\ref{sec:c5:appendix-prompts}.

\subsection{Direct Supervised Fine-Tuning}
\label{sec:c5:direct-sft}

Direct SFT trains Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct through autoregressive next-token prediction. Let $y_1,\ldots,y_T$ denote the tokenised ground-truth JSON response, $I_{\mathrm{prompt}}$ the mask-based visual prompt, and $x_{\mathrm{text}}$ the textual instruction. The supervised objective is the mean token-level cross-entropy
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SFT}}
    =-\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}
    \log p_{\theta}\!\left(y_t\mid I_{\mathrm{prompt}},x_{\mathrm{text}},y_{<t}\right).
    \label{eq:c5:sft-loss}
\end{equation}
During training, each response token is predicted while the preceding ground-truth response tokens $y_{<t}$ are supplied as context, the standard procedure known as teacher forcing. Only assistant-response tokens contribute to the loss; textual instruction tokens are masked. The structured labels are therefore learned through their textual serialisation rather than through a separate classification head or a differentiable JSON parser. Gradients update the QLoRA adapters, whereas response parsing and metric computation are used only during evaluation.

The base model is loaded with 4-bit NF4 quantisation and bfloat16 computation. QLoRA adapters are applied to the attention and MLP projection layers, with rank $r=64$, scaling factor $\alpha=128$, and dropout $0.05$; the vision encoder, multimodal projector, and base language model remain frozen.

Direct SFT predicts the verb and target jointly in a single response, although the target is often conditional on the action being performed. This motivates testing whether the prediction can be made easier by first identifying the verb and then using it when predicting the target. The visual evidence itself can also be ambiguous. Figure~\ref{fig:c5:adaptation-motivation} shows examples in which local anatomy, temporal context, or knowledge of valid label relationships may help to distinguish plausible predictions. A final adaptation tests whether supervision from a larger model can make the relevant visual evidence more explicit. These hypotheses motivate the controlled adaptations considered next.

\subsection{Interaction-Prediction Adaptations}
\label{sec:c5:adaptations}

Each method changes one aspect of the Stage~2 task, input, or supervision while retaining the principal Direct SFT optimisation configuration.

\subsubsection{Sequential Dialogue Prediction}

Sequential Dialogue Prediction tests whether decomposing joint interaction prediction into smaller conditional subtasks makes the problem easier for the model. Following the divide-and-discern motivation of dialogue tuning~\cite{zhang2026bootstrapping}, it breaks the prediction process into three successive question--response exchanges rather than requesting the complete triplet immediately. The first question requests one verb for each grounded instance. The second requests the corresponding targets while retaining the verb response in the conversation. A final question requests the complete \ivttriplet response using the accumulated dialogue context. This staged formulation is intended to guide the model through the constituent decisions before producing the final structured prediction; the experiment evaluates its effect on interaction-prediction performance rather than independently validating the model's reasoning process. During training, the reference responses to preceding questions are supplied, and the token-level cross-entropy in Equation~\ref{eq:c5:sft-loss} is applied to the response tokens from all three exchanges. During inference, the questions are asked sequentially and each generated response remains in the conversation before the next question is posed. The final response is parsed and evaluated using the same identifier and label checks as Direct SFT. Consequently, an early prediction can affect the subsequent responses. The model is trained independently from the same public base model as Direct SFT, with otherwise identical QLoRA and optimisation settings. The complete dialogue templates and response schemas are provided in Section~\ref{sec:c5:additional-details}.

\subsubsection{Temporal Visual Context}

Several surgical actions are easier to distinguish from motion or from a change in instrument--tissue contact than from a single image. Temporal Visual Context therefore tests whether immediately neighbouring frames provide evidence that is absent from the centre frame alone. The three-frame model uses $(t-1,t,t+1)$ and the five-frame model uses $(t-2,t-1,t,t+1,t+2)$ as an ordered native multimodal input, where neighbouring inputs are immediately adjacent source-video frames. Every frame contains overlays derived from its own instrument masks; no temporal tracking or mask propagation is used. The textual instruction explicitly identifies the current centre frame as the prediction target and states that the neighbouring frames provide context only. Consequently, only the centre frame supplies triplet supervision. Windows remain within the same video and split, and at video boundaries only available neighbouring frames are used.

The total visual-token budget is held equal to the single-frame setting and is therefore shared across the temporal frames. The comparison between three and five frames is consequently exploratory rather than a controlled temporal-window ablation: the five-frame input contains more visual content within the same budget. Apart from the temporal input, training and validation follow the Direct SFT configuration.

\subsubsection{Anatomical Context Priors}

The target of an interaction is usually determined by the anatomy close to the working end of the instrument, yet neighbouring structures can be difficult to distinguish from RGB appearance alone. Anatomical Context Priors therefore test whether a coarse description of local anatomy can guide target prediction. The instrument-tip position is not provided by CholecTriplet-Seg, so it is estimated from the instance mask using a heuristic. A more direct localisation approach could in future use skeletal pose annotations such as those introduced by ROBUST-MIPS~\cite{han2025robust}; this was not part of the present experiments.

The method uses the same EndoViT checkpoint and label space described in Chapter~\ref{chap:triplet-grounding}. The heuristic first estimates the laparoscopic field boundary and identifies instrument-mask pixels close to either that boundary or an image edge. Their mean position defines the estimated instrument base, and the mask pixel furthest from this base defines the distal tip. A circular neighbourhood centred on the endpoint has radius one-eighth of the smaller image dimension. Instrument pixels are excluded from this neighbourhood. Each remaining pixel is assigned its highest-probability EndoViT class; classes are ranked by their proportion of the valid region, and the three most prevalent classes and proportions are appended as text to the textual instruction. The mask-based visual prompt itself is unchanged. Anatomical context is supplied during both training and inference. The model is trained independently from the same base model with the Direct SFT configuration. The complete heuristic and prior format are documented in Section~\ref{sec:c5:additional-details}.

\subsubsection{Ontology-Guided Prompting}

Not every verb--target pair is valid for every instrument: the CholecTriplet-Seg label space contains 100 valid triplets rather than the full Cartesian product of its instrument, verb, and target classes. Ontology-Guided Prompting tests whether stating these admissible relationships helps the model avoid plausible-sounding but invalid combinations. It augments the textual instruction with the valid \vtpair combinations for each supplied instrument class, taken without modification from the ontology introduced in Chapter~\ref{chap:triplet-grounding}. Ontology information is included during both fine-tuning and evaluation, but decoding remains autoregressive and unconstrained. The experiment therefore evaluates prompt-level access to the ontology rather than constrained prediction.

\subsubsection{Distilled-Reasoning Supervision}

Direct SFT supervises only the final structured answer and does not explicitly identify the visual evidence that supports it. Distilled-Reasoning Supervision tests whether explanation traces generated by a larger model can provide a richer learning signal, following prior work on learning from model-generated rationales and explanation traces~\cite{hsieh2023distilling,mukherjee2023orca}. Qwen3.7-Max, a closed-weight commercial teacher model used as a fixed model through its API, was prompted with the grounded image and ground-truth labels to generate a structured rationale for each instance. Each response describes contact evidence, action evidence, a primary target hypothesis, a contrastive target hypothesis, uncertainty, and the final triplet. For example, one rationale identifies contact between a hook and a dark tissue fold, contrasts omentum with gallbladder as candidate targets, records the visual uncertainty, and ends with the reference triplet \texttt{Monopolar Hook--dissect--omentum}. The complete example is provided in Section~\ref{sec:c5:appendix-rationale-example}.

Rationales were generated for all splits, but only training-split rationales were used as supervision. Approximately ten rationales were inspected informally; they appeared plausible, but neither systematic quality control nor clinical expert validation was performed.

\begin{figure}[htb!]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{figures/distilled_reasoning_supervision.pdf}
    \caption{Distilled-reasoning supervision. Qwen3.7-Max generates structured rationales from grounded images and ground-truth triplets. A Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct student is then trained to reproduce the rationale and final structured prediction.}
    \label{fig:c5:distilled-reasoning}
\end{figure}

For Distilled Rationale Only training, the teacher-generated rationale and its final structured prediction together form the reference model response. The student is trained to reproduce this complete response using the same token-level cross-entropy as Equation~\ref{eq:c5:sft-loss}. At evaluation, the student generates both parts autoregressively, after which only the final structured triplet is parsed and scored. The multitask model instead uses equal numbers of concise Direct SFT examples and distilled-rationale examples. The same trained multitask model is evaluated in two modes: \emph{Multitask---Direct SFT} requests the concise response, whereas \emph{Multitask---Distilled Rationale} requests the rationale before the final prediction. Every supervised response token has the same weight in the loss. Since the rationale contains substantially more tokens than the final answer, it contributes a larger share of the Distilled Rationale Only objective; no separate answer-token weighting was used. All student models otherwise follow the Direct SFT configuration.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\label{sec:c5:experimental-protocol}

\subsection{Dataset and Samples}

All experiments use the official CholecTriplet-Seg training, validation, and test partitions introduced in Chapter~\ref{chap:triplet-grounding}: 22,818 training frames from 45 videos, 2,496 validation frames from one video, and 5,641 test frames from four videos. The chapter reuses the established annotations without further relabelling or cleaning. Frames containing no visible instrument instance are excluded because Stage~2 predicts interactions only for grounded instances. Each single-frame sample contains all grounded instances and associated triplets in one annotated frame. All adaptations use the same data partitions as Direct SFT.

\subsection{Evaluation}

Performance is measured using the mask-based IVTMetrics segmentation protocol defined in Chapter~\ref{chap:triplet-grounding}. The reported measures are instrument AP (AP$_I$), verb AP (AP$_V$), target AP (AP$_T$), instrument--verb AP (AP$_{IV}$), instrument--target AP (AP$_{IT}$), and complete-triplet AP (AP$_{IVT}$). The same matching, overlap, assignment, and class-averaging rules are used here.

Ground-truth grounding fixes the instrument masks, classes, and instance count; AP$_I$ is therefore 100 by construction, while the remaining metrics are computed from Stage~2 outputs. Predicted grounding uses the fixed Stage~1 detector and includes both grounding and interaction errors. Only Direct SFT is evaluated with predicted instances; all adaptations are evaluated under ground-truth grounding. Small differences between configurations are therefore interpreted as observations rather than definitive improvements.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:c5:results}

\subsection{Interaction Prediction with Ground-Truth Grounding}

\tabref{tab:c5:principal-adaptations} reports the principal comparison under ground-truth grounding. Direct SFT reaches AP$_{IVT}=14.56$, establishing that the MLLM can perform structured interaction prediction when instrument instances are supplied. Three-frame temporal context gives the highest observed AP$_{IVT}$ at 15.89, an absolute increase of 1.33 points, and also gives the highest AP$_V$ and AP$_{IV}$. Sequential Dialogue Prediction reaches AP$_{IVT}=15.31$, 0.75 points above Direct SFT, despite slightly lower component metrics. These differences are promising observations, but the available evidence does not establish definitive superiority.

\subsection{Complete Two-Stage System}

\tabref{tab:c5:predicted-grounding} evaluates Direct SFT with Stage~1 predictions and compares it with the \chapref{chap:triplet-grounding} systems under the same dataset and IVTMetrics mask-segmentation protocol. \section{Discussion}
\label{sec:c5:discussion}

\subsection{Viability of the Two-Stage Formulation}

The principal finding is that an MLLM can act as a viable second-stage interaction predictor when instrument instances are supplied. Direct SFT requires no explicit rationale, anatomical prompt, or ontology constraint, yet provides a strong reference point under ground-truth grounding and remains competitive in the complete predicted-grounding pipeline. The decomposition focuses the MLLM on semantic interaction prediction while leaving dense localisation to a specialist model. This does not prove that two-stage modelling is universally preferable to end-to-end learning, but it establishes the formulation as credible and exposes the contribution and failure modes of each stage more clearly.

The complete-system results also clarify the relationship to TargetFusionNet. Component-level scores are substantially stronger for the two-stage Direct SFT pipeline, but AP$_{IVT}$ is essentially unchanged. Complete-triplet evaluation is conjunctive: better verb or target recognition contributes only when localisation, instrument identity, and the remaining semantic component are also correct for the same instance. Consequently, component gains should not be presented as equivalent to complete interaction gains.

\subsection{Task Factorisation and Temporal Evidence}

Sequential Dialogue Prediction and three-frame context produce the strongest observed AP$_{IVT}$ results among the principal Stage~2 experiments. Their effects are nevertheless metric-specific. Sequential prediction increases AP$_{IVT}$ while slightly decreasing every reported component or pair metric relative to Direct SFT. It may encourage more coherent complete predictions, but the present experiment does not quantify error propagation across dialogue turns, and the difference between teacher-forced training responses and generated inference responses remains important.

Three-frame input gives a larger AP$_{IVT}$ increase and modest improvements in AP$_V$ and AP$_{IV}$. This is consistent with the premise that neighbouring frames can reveal motion or interaction progression absent from a still image. However, the experiment uses ground-truth grounding and only one seed. The five-frame result further shows that additional visual context is not automatically useful when a fixed token budget is divided among more frames. It should not be interpreted as a controlled demonstration that three frames are intrinsically optimal.

\subsection{Why Additional Priors and Longer Supervision Did Not Consistently Help}

The anatomical and ontology experiments provide useful negative evidence about prompt-level integration. EndoViT context is conceptually connected to the weak anatomical priors investigated in Chapter~\ref{chap:triplet-grounding}, but the present conversion of dense predictions into three textual class proportions does not improve AP$_T$. This result does not show that anatomy is unhelpful; it shows that this heuristic tip region and prompt representation do not reliably improve the final task.

Similarly, access to valid \vtpair combinations does not improve AP$_{IVT}$. Because generation remains unconstrained, the model may not consistently use the supplied ontology. The experiment therefore evaluates contextual guidance, not the potential value of constrained decoding.

Distilled-reasoning supervision also fails to outperform Direct SFT. The rationales were generated at scale but were not clinically validated, and only a small informal sample was inspected. Equal weighting also directs most response-token loss towards reproducing the rationale rather than the evaluated triplet. These design properties provide plausible explanations for the negative result, but their individual effects were not tested and should not be treated as established causes.

Across these experiments, anatomical targets remain the principal semantic challenge. Dense target annotation could provide more direct spatial supervision than the weak local priors evaluated here, but delineating interacting anatomy requires substantial expert effort and is often ambiguous at instrument boundaries. Moreover, the annotation burden grows as additional procedures introduce new structures and interaction ontologies. Dense target labels are therefore valuable where they can be obtained, but are unlikely to provide the only scalable route to grounded interaction prediction.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:c5:limitations}

The study has four principal limitations. First, only Direct SFT is evaluated with predicted grounding. The sequential, temporal, anatomical, ontology, and distilled-supervision variants are assessed only with ground-truth instances, so their behaviour in the complete system is unknown.

Second, adaptations are evaluated independently. No controlled combinations test whether sequential factorisation and temporal context are complementary, and the reported differences have not been subjected to formal uncertainty analysis.

Third, malformed generations and verb-to-target error propagation were not quantified systematically. Invalid outputs receive no credit, but their frequency may differ between formulations and remains an unmeasured source of variability. Finally, all experiments use CholecTriplet-Seg and laparoscopic cholecystectomy. Generalisation to other procedures, acquisition conditions, and interaction ontologies has not been demonstrated.

\section{Future Research}
\label{sec:c5:future-work}

The immediate priority is to evaluate the strongest Stage~2 variants with predicted Stage~1 grounding, thereby testing whether their observed advantages persist in the complete pipeline. Temporal predicted grounding may require instance association or mask propagation because independently detected instances are not guaranteed to correspond across frames. More explicit temporal integration may also use motion and procedural continuity more effectively than distributing a fixed visual budget across independent images. Subsequent comparative studies should include repeated runs and controlled combinations of the strongest adaptations.

Sequential prediction should be tested with imperfect verb conditioning during training so that robustness to first-turn errors can be measured directly. Controlled combinations of the strongest adaptations would determine whether task factorisation and temporal evidence are complementary. Curriculum-style training could also initialise specialised variants from a converged Direct SFT adapter rather than training each independently from the base model.

For distilled supervision, future work should prioritise rationale quality control and objectives that increase the relative weight of final answer tokens. Comparison-based or contrastive auxiliary tasks may offer a more directly verifiable form of supervision than long free-text rationales. Finally, evaluation on additional procedures and datasets is required to establish whether two-stage interaction prediction transfers beyond cholecystectomy. ProstaTD, a fully supervised surgical triplet-detection dataset for robot-assisted prostatectomy, provides a relevant external benchmark for investigating this cross-procedure generalisation~\cite{prostatd}.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:c5:conclusion}

This chapter formulates surgical triplet segmentation as instrument instance segmentation followed by MLLM-based interaction prediction. Direct SFT establishes a viable Stage~2 baseline. When ground-truth instrument instances are provided, Sequential Dialogue Prediction and three-frame temporal context produce promising observed gains in AP$_{IVT}$, while prompt-level anatomical priors, ontology guidance, five-frame context, and distilled-reasoning supervision show that additional context or longer supervision does not inherently improve interaction prediction. The mixed component-level effects require the relative differences between adaptations to be interpreted cautiously.

When the full system uses predicted masks, grounding errors reduce performance. Nevertheless, the two-stage Direct SFT pipeline produces substantially stronger component scores and complete-triplet AP comparable to TargetFusionNet. The results therefore establish two-stage multimodal interaction prediction as a viable culmination of the thesis's technical progression: Chapter~\ref{chap:cholecinstanceseg} establishes the spatial substrate, Chapter~\ref{chap:triplet-grounding} connects that substrate to grounded interactions, and this chapter demonstrates that semantic interaction prediction can be studied as a specialised second stage. The resulting framework provides a coherent basis for continued research without making the contribution demonstrated here dependent on additional experiments.
