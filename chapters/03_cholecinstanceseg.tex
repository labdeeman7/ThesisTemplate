\section*{Chapter Introduction}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Chapter Introduction}

The literature review in the preceding chapter identifies spatially precise surgical scene understanding as an important foundation for computer-assisted intervention. Instrument presence or semantic segmentation alone cannot distinguish multiple instances of the same instrument class, limiting the ability to associate individual tools with their actions and anatomical targets.

This chapter addresses that data limitation through CholecInstanceSeg, an instance-segmentation dataset derived from routine human laparoscopic cholecystectomy. Within the thesis, the dataset provides the spatial supervision needed for the subsequent progression from instrument localisation to grounded instrument--verb--target interactions.

The main body reproduces the accepted manuscript of Alabi et al., \emph{CholecInstanceSeg: A Tool Instance Segmentation Dataset for Laparoscopic Surgery}. Thesis integration is limited to the removal of standalone publisher machinery, use of thesis-compatible headings and figure paths, and harmonisation with the canonical thesis bibliography. The accepted scientific text, results, tables, figures, equations, and conclusions are otherwise preserved.

\section*{Chapter Summary and Transition}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Chapter Summary and Transition}

CholecInstanceSeg contributes instance-level instrument annotations at a scale and clinical diversity intended to support robust surgical-tool localisation. Its construction, quality-control process, recommended partitions, and baseline validation establish both a reusable dataset and an empirical foundation for the remaining technical chapters of this thesis.

The next chapter uses this spatial foundation to move beyond identifying instruments in isolation. It links individual instrument masks with surgical verbs and anatomical targets, formulating surgical action-triplet understanding as an instance-level grounding and segmentation problem.
