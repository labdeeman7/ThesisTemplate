\section*{Chapter Introduction}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Chapter Introduction}

The literature review in the preceding chapter identifies a missing spatial link in fine-grained surgical activity understanding. Frame-level instrument and action labels can describe what is present, while semantic segmentation can localise an instrument class, but neither necessarily distinguishes multiple physical instances of that class. Without this distinction, actions and anatomical targets cannot be assigned unambiguously to the individual instruments responsible for them.

This chapter addresses that data limitation through CholecInstanceSeg, an instance-segmentation dataset derived from routine human laparoscopic cholecystectomy. Within the thesis, the dataset provides the spatial supervision needed for the subsequent progression from instrument localisation to grounded \ivttriplet interaction prediction.

This chapter is based on the accepted paper entitled \emph{CholecInstanceSeg: A Tool Instance Segmentation Dataset for Laparoscopic Surgery} by Alabi et al., published in \emph{Scientific Data} (2025). Its main body reproduces the accepted manuscript, followed by its accompanying annotation-guideline supplementary material. Thesis integration is limited to the removal of standalone publisher machinery, use of thesis-compatible headings and figure paths, and harmonisation with the canonical thesis bibliography. The accepted scientific text, results, tables, figures, equations, conclusions, and substantive supplementary content are otherwise preserved.

\section*{Chapter Summary}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Chapter Summary}
\markright{Chapter Summary}

CholecInstanceSeg contributes instance-level instrument annotations across a large and clinically diverse collection of laparoscopic cholecystectomy videos. Its construction, quality-control process, recommended partitions, and baseline validation establish both a reusable dataset and the spatial foundation for the remaining technical chapters. The chapter demonstrates how individual instruments can be distinguished and localised, but localisation alone does not describe what each instrument is doing or which anatomy it affects.

The next chapter therefore asks whether semantic interaction labels can be attached to these spatially resolved instances. It links individual instrument masks with surgical verbs and anatomical targets, transforming surgical action-triplet understanding from frame-level recognition into an instance-level grounding and segmentation problem.
